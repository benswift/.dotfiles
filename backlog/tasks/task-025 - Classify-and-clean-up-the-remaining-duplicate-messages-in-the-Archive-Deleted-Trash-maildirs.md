## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
After the August 2026 mailsync investigation, 1248 redundant message copies remain across the maildirs. The two causes already fixed are not among them: neomutt now flushes maildir flag changes on an idle timeout (commit 0d31ef5), and the Exchange accounts no longer Fcc sent mail (2a1b9e1), whose 1910 duplicates were cleaned up at the time.

What remains has not been characterised, and splits into at least two populations.

About 372 carry the "half-move" fingerprint: a copy generated by neomutt (filename form `<time>.R<bignum>.<host>`) paired with a copy pulled down by mbsync (`<time>.<pid>_<seq>.<host>`), same Message-ID, same folder. These are historical damage from the bug fixed in 0d31ef5 --- `save-message` wrote the Archive copy to disk immediately but deferred deleting the source, so a mailsync landing in that window uploaded the copy and left the original. They are concentrated in anu/Archive (237), personal/Archive (132) and phdconvenor/Archive (3).

The other ~876 do not match that pattern and their cause is unknown. personal/Archive holds 147k messages accumulated over many years, so a fair number are plausibly legitimate --- ordinary resends, forwards of one's own mail, or messages filed twice deliberately. They must not be assumed to be damage.

Current per-folder counts (extra copies beyond the first, then how many sets show the half-move pair):

    anu/Archive          3156 msgs   252 extra   237 half-move
    anu/Deleted Items    1559 msgs    56 extra     6 half-move
    personal/Archive   147058 msgs   820 extra   132 half-move
    personal/Junk Mail   1670 msgs     5 extra     0 half-move
    personal/Trash         61 msgs     4 extra     4 half-move
    phdconvenor/Archive   225 msgs     5 extra     3 half-move

The Sent Items rule used in the earlier cleanup does not transfer. There the discriminator was structural and total: one copy had no transport headers at all because neomutt wrote it before submission, the other had the full Received chain, and every one of the 1910 sets fit. Two copies of the same archived message have both been through the transport, so both carry Received headers and the pair may be byte-identical. A different discriminator is needed, and the safe-set may well be smaller than the duplicate set.

Note the repo has prior history here: two completed tasks, "Nuclear cleanup of ANU Archive maildir" and "Restore and deduplicate ANU archive emails". Read them before starting --- they may explain some of the unclassified population.

Deleting a copy locally causes mbsync to expunge it from the server too (Expunge Both is set on every channel), so any cleanup is a live change to Fastmail and Exchange, not just to local disk. The quarantine-sync-verify pattern used for the Sent Items cleanup worked well and is the model to follow: move candidates out of ~/Maildir with a manifest recording each original path, sync, then verify every affected Message-ID still resolves to exactly one surviving copy before anything is deleted for good.
